# Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Movable Antenna-aided Cell-Free Massive MIMO Systems 

Bokai Xu, Jiayi Zhang, Senior Member, IEEE , Shuaifei Chen, Member, IEEE , Ziheng Liu, Huahua Xiao, Derrick Wing Kwan Ng, Fellow, IEEE , and Bo Ai, Fellow, IEEE 

Abstract —The inherent non-convex minimum-separation con-straints introduced by movable antennas present a formidable challenge to the joint optimization of antenna positions and transmission strategies, rendering conventional methods com-putationally infeasible, particularly in large-scale cell-free mas-sive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO). In this work, we propose the graph-based learning individual intrinsic re-ward heterogeneous-agent proximal policy optimization (GLIIR-HAPPO) algorithm, a novel heterogeneous multi-agent reinforce-ment learning (MARL) framework that fundamentally over-comes this impasse by systematically decomposing the original coupled optimization into coordinated subproblems. To ensure tractability, we embed the non-convex geometric constraints into a penalty-augmented reward structure and develop a specialized geometric solver that enables the positioning agents to effi-ciently navigate the high-dimensional action space. Specifically, we propose an architecture featuring a dynamic-interaction graph critic for adaptive cross-role coordination, together with role-conditioned federated distillation that synchronizes same-role policies through compact actor-output statistics. Beyond architectural design, we establish a rigorous theoretical anal-ysis that derives monotonic performance improvement bounds and establishes convergence guarantees for the proposed bi-level optimization. Numerical simulations demonstrate that our framework yields significant sum-rate improvements over state-of-the-art MARL schemes. Moreover, the performance of our advanced architecture closely approaches its fully centralized counterpart, while drastically reducing communication overhead. 

Index Terms —Movable antenna (MA), cell-free massive MIMO, multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL). 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Recently, the proliferation of wireless applications has led to an unprecedented surge in demand for higher through-put, improved spectral efficiency, and enhanced network ca-pacity for future wireless communication systems beyond fifth-generation (5G) [1]–[3]. Various emerging bandwidth-intensive applications such as high-quality video streaming and immersive virtual/augmented reality (VR/AR) have sig-nificantly contributed to this escalating demand. Recognizing this trend, researchers and engineers are directing their efforts towards the development of sixth-generation (6G) wireless communication systems and beyond [4], [5]. In particular, multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) communications have emerged as a foundational enabling technology for achieving these performance targets. However, in traditional cellular

MIMO systems, users located at the cell edge often face severely degraded communication quality due to severe path loss, strong inter-cell interference, and limited spatial diversity. The concept of cell-free (CF) networks, which eliminates the limitations imposed by traditional cell boundaries by coordinating a large number of geographically distributed access points (APs), has gained significant traction as a highly promising solution for improving network-wide throughput and enhancing a more uniform user experience [6]. How-ever, while CF MIMO technology can significantly improve both wireless channel capacity and system spectral efficiency (SE), it often entails non-negligible implementation com-plexity and increased energy consumption due to the need for numerous RF chains, dense AP deployments, and sub-stantial fronthaul signaling/processing overhead. Furthermore, the desirable channel-hardening phenomenon manifests only under specific propagation and deployment regimes, such as scenarios with a relatively small path-loss exponent. It is also worth noting that favorable propagation characteristics are exhibited only among users that are well separated in space [7], which can limit the achievable spatial multiplexing gains in practical networks with heterogeneous and potentially clustered user distributions. To address the limitations in CF massive MIMO systems, researchers have put forth various promising technologies. One such approach is antenna selection (AS), which reduces energy consumption by activating only those antennas that contribute the most to system throughput. However, AS is inherently lim-ited to a finite, discrete set of pre-deployed antenna locations. To unlock the full potential of the spatial domain, the concept of flexible-position antenna systems has emerged. A prominent realization of this concept is the fluid antenna system (FAS), which enables the flexible adjustment of radiating elements. The fluid antenna multiple access (FAMA) framework, for instance, empowers user-equipment devices (UEs) to exploit naturally occurring interference nulls by dynamically varying the antenna position, thereby establishing additional spatial de-grees of freedom. In practice, this capability can be realized via liquid-based reconfigurable radiators or by switching among densely packed antenna ports [8], [9]. Parallel to the advancements in FAS, movable antenna (MA) technology has recently attracted considerable attention as another practical and robust implementation for realizing flexible-position MIMO [10], [11]. While FAS effectively leverages spatial diversity through reconfigurable materials or port switching, MA systems typically utilize localized elec-tromechanical drivers to continuously translate solid radiating elements within a prescribed region. In this work, we adopt the MA architecture for CF APs due to several specific advantages that align with our system design: First, MA can effectively arXiv:2610.08387v1 [cs.IT] 6 Oct 2026 2

leverage the available spatial DoF to its fullest potential by dynamically adjusting antenna positions, thereby improving SE. Second, MA can enhance channel gains by exploiting only a reduced number of repositionable antennas, leading to lower hardware requirements and enhanced energy efficiency (EE). Finally, MA can be relocated to spatial locations where multi-user channels become more orthogonal, facilitating the establishment of favorable propagation conditions. To unlock the full potential of wireless systems, optimizing transmission schemes is of utmost importance. In particular, MA-aided MIMO systems pose a significant challenge due to their highly coupled and geometric-dependent constraints. Such tightly intertwined spatial and signal-domain relation-ships further complicate the underlying optimization task. For instance, in [12], the authors proposed an efficient alternating-optimization algorithm that iteratively optimizes the transmit covariance matrix and the positions of each transmit/receive MA, yielding a locally optimal but computationally intensive solution. Compared to traditional fixed-position antenna (FPA) systems, MA systems achieve significant gains in MIMO channel capacity due to their spatial flexibility. Another study [13] employed a gradient-descent method to jointly optimize the positions of MAs for all users. However, from an optimiza-tion standpoint, MA-aided systems introduce inherently non-convex minimum-distance (collision-avoidance) constraints and may also involve non-convex objective functions. Indeed, these factors present challenges in solving the underlying optimization problems, especially at scale. Existing research addresses these challenges through various approaches, such as successive convex approximation (SCA) [12] or compressed sensing (CS)-based techniques [14]. Nevertheless, these meth-ods typically rely on surrogate convexifications of the objective and/or the non-convex distance constraints, or they introduce additional non-convex constraints (e.g., l0-type sparsity con-straints), which can incur higher computational burden and may lead to performance degradation. Additionally, existing research on optimizing MA designs has primarily focused on centralized optimization algorithms. These approaches require the sharing of CSI and other in-formation among all BSs, typically through a centralized design at the central processing unit (CPU). However, this centralized approach incurs substantial fronthaul signaling and data-exchange overhead [15]. In real-world deployments, BSs cannot engage in frequent message exchanges due to stringent fronthaul bandwidth/latency constraints and limited link capacity, making iterative centralized procedures less fa-vorable. This raises a crucial question: How can we effectively optimize the transmission strategies of multiple BSs with minimal or even no message exchanges [16]? In the realm of distributed optimization, multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) has emerged as a highly promising framework with rapidly growing adoption [17]. In practice, MARL naturally complements the distributed nature of cell-free mMIMO sys-tems by enabling multiple agents (e.g., mobile-BSs or UEs) to learn coordinated policies from local observations and optimize their strategies collaboratively. Under centralized training with decentralized execution, agents can use aggre-gated information during training and local observations for online policy execution. However, conventional MARL-based schemes face fundamental challenges, including poor sample efficiency, limited scalability, and unstable learning dynamics, due to non-stationarity and difficult credit assignment when agents are driven solely by a global team reward. Therefore, it is necessary to design a decoupled learning and coordination architecture to improve the robustness and performance of MARL [18]. In addition to these algorithmic challenges, existing literature has yet to adequately resolve several critical implementation difficulties: 1) the computational intractability arising from non-convex, geometry-coupled constraints; 2) the prohibitive communication overhead required for effective coordination in large-scale networks; and 3) the amplified learning instability when heterogeneous agents attempt to collaborate under sparse global rewards. To fill in the above gap, in this paper, we address the fol-lowing fundamental question: How can we design a heteroge-neous multi-agent optimization framework that simultaneously resolves the geometric constraints of antenna positioning and the interference management of power control? 

This work investigates the joint optimization of MA po-sitioning and power allocation under the adopted channel and geometric models. Problem (P) maximizes the nom-inal sum rate subject to power and geometric constraints, whereas the MARL extrinsic team reward combines the nom-inal sum rate with a geometric feasibility penalty. Practical MA non-idealities, including movement delay, actuation en-ergy, positioning uncertainty, finite movement resolution, and CSI/control overhead, are considered through simplified mod-eling and supplementary sensitivity analysis. Our contributions are summarized as follows.  

> •

We introduce a novel heterogeneous multi-agent collab-oration learning framework, powered by a graph-based learning individual intrinsic reward heterogeneous-agent proximal policy optimization (GLIIR-HAPPO) algorithm to overcome the intractability of monolithic central-ized optimizers in MA-aided CF-MIMO networks. This paradigm fundamentally decomposes the joint optimiza-tion of antenna positions and transmission strategies into role-specialized subtasks, handled respectively by MA-positioning agents and power-control agents, enabling role-specialized policy learning with centralized training and decentralized online execution.  

> •

We tackle the intractable non-convex minimum-distance constraints between antennas through a dual strategy. First, a penalty-augmented mechanism is integrated into the reward function to drive the learning process toward constraint satisfaction. Second, a specialized geometric solver empowers positioning agents to efficiently navigate the high-dimensional action space and identify feasible, collision-free locations, transforming an otherwise in-tractable geometric problem into a manageable optimiza-tion task.  

> •

To reduce learning-related coordination overhead, we combine a dynamic-interaction graph critic with role-conditioned federated distillation. B. Heterogeneous Coordination Network 

The heterogeneous MARL schemes developed in [23]–[25] can solve distributed optimization efficiently. However, they do not explicitly capture inter-AP information exchange and do not scale gracefully with increasing agent population, which can lead to prohibitive interaction overhead in large networks. To address this limitation, we propose a heterogeneous co-ordination architecture that integrates a dynamic-interaction graph critic with a LIIR module that learns a distinct intrinsic reward for each AP agent. VI. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we proposed a novel heterogeneous MARL framework to address the joint optimization of antenna place-ment and transmission in MA-aided cell-free massive MIMO systems. Our approach decomposes the underlying non-convex design into role-specialized subproblems, which are handled by dedicated agents. Specifically, we handle the strict geomet-ric constraints through a combination of variable splitting with quadratic penalties and feasibility-aware reward shaping, en-abling tractable distributed learning under minimum-distance requirements. Scalable and efficient coordination is achieved via a dynamic-interaction graph critic and role-conditioned federated distillation, which together provide coordinated de-cision making with low communication overhead. Numerical results demonstrate that the proposed framework delivers substantial sum-rate gains over conventional FPA systems and achieves near-centralized performance while significantly re-ducing communication overhead. Moreover, the stability of the 15 

proposed approach is supported by trust-region analysis and two-timescale meta-gradient convergence arguments. Future work will integrate movement delay, actuation energy, posi-tioning uncertainty, finite resolution, and CSI/control overhead into a hardware-aware objective and training reward, with model calibration and validation through over-the-air (OTA) or hardware experiments.
