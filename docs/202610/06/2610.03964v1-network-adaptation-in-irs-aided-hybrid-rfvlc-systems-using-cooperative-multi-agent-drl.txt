# Network Adaptation in IRS-Aided Hybrid RF/VLC Systems Using Cooperative Multi-Agent DRL 

Ahrar N. Hamad, Ahmad Adnan Qidan, Taisir E.H. El-Gorashi and Jaafar M. H. Elmirghani, 

Department of Engineering, King’s College London, London, United Kingdom, 

{ahrar.hamad, ahmad.qidan, taisir.elgorashi, jaafar.elmirghani }@kcl.ac.uk 

Abstract —Hybrid radio frequency (RF) and visible light communication (VLC) networks have emerged as a promising solution for high-capacity indoor wireless connectivity in sixth-generation (6G) systems. However, the limited optical coverage and vulnerability of VLC links to line-of-sight (LoS) blockage under user mobility remain fundamental challenges. In this work, a mirror-based intelligent reflecting surface (IRS) is deployed to assist a dynamic indoor hybrid RF/VLC network, where each mobile user is exclusively assigned to either the IRS-enhanced VLC subnetwork or the RF subnetwork through a binary selection decision. A joint optimization problem is then formulated to maximize proportional fairness by jointly optimizing the RF/VLC technology selection, power allocation, and IRS mirror roll and yaw orientation angles. To enable real-time adaptability, the problem is reformulated as a Markov decision process (MDP) and solved using a cooperative multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (DRL) algorithm based on centralized training with decentralized execution. Simulation results demonstrate the superior performance of the optimized hybrid network compared with optimized standalone VLC and RF networks. The results further validate the practicality and effectiveness of the proposed DRL framework compared to widely adopted DRL algorithms as well as conventional model-based optimization approaches. 

Index Terms —6G, visible light communication (VLC), radio frequency (RF), intelligent reflecting surface (IRS), deep rein-forcement learning (DRL). 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Visible light communication (VLC) is an attractive option for simultaneously delivering high-speed data transmission and illumination through optical access points (APs), while reusing the existing lighting infrastructure to align with the green communication objectives of sixth-generation (6G) networks. It offers access to the unregulated optical spectrum, high spectral efficiency, and complete immunity to radio-frequency (RF) interference. Therefore, VLC can be integrated with conventional RF networks to form hybrid RF/VLC systems, offering higher network capacity, enhanced reliability, and im-proved energy efficiency [1]. However, VLC systems still face fundamental coverage limitations, as the coverage footprint of optical APs is spatially constrained, while line-of-sight (LoS) links are highly susceptible to blockage by physical objects and user mobility [2], [3]. Intelligent reflecting surface (IRS) has emerged as an effective solution for enhancing connectivity in indoor VLC systems without consuming sig-nificant power [4]. Specifically, a steerable IRS mirror array can overcome LoS blockage by dynamically redirecting non-LoS (NLoS) optical signals toward mobile users, effectively extending the optical AP coverage footprint and improving the achievable data rate [5]. Hybrid RF/VLC networks introduce complex optimization challenges involving technology selection and resource al-location. In [6], alternating optimization was employed to jointly optimize user association and power allocation in a hy-brid RF/VLC system using successive convex approximations (SCA). Similarly, in [7], a joint AP assignment and power allocation problem was formulated as a combinatorial opti-mization problem to maximize the sum rate and solved using a matching theory-based distributed algorithm combined with a heuristic power allocation scheme. However, these approaches treat the VLC channel as fixed and uncontrollable, limiting their ability to adapt to dynamic propagation conditions. Building on the controllable NLoS capability enabled by IRSs, the work in [8] employed a sine-cosine algorithm to determine the optimal IRS mirror configuration for establishing reliable NLoS links. Nevertheless, such model-based optimization ap-proaches typically rely on accurate channel knowledge and require repeated optimization whenever channel conditions change, making them less suitable for real-time adaptation in dynamic environments with user mobility. Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has recently emerged as an effective framework for solving optimization problems including integer variables, coupled continuous actions, and non-convex objectives [9]. Unlike model-based methods, a DRL agent learns an adaptive policy through interaction with the environment, enabling real-time response to mobility and blockage. In [10], a deep Q-network (DQN) algorithm jointly optimized user association, bandwidth, and power in a hybrid RF/VLC network. However, its discrete action space cannot capture continuous control variables. In [11], such challenges were addressed using on-policy algorithms for resource al-location and load balancing in hybrid RF/VLC systems. In [12], a deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG) algorithm is used to optimize the transmit power among the users in the hybrid RF/VLC system. Despite these advances, existing works do not jointly consider IRS reconfiguration, technology selection, and power allocation, particularly in dynamic IRS-aided hybrid RF/VLC scenarios involving user mobility. Motivated by these gaps, this paper proposes a novel frame-work for jointly optimizing RF/VLC link selection, power allocation, and IRS mirror orientation in a dynamic multi-user indoor environment. We consider a downlink indoor IRS-assisted hybrid RF/VLC system, as shown in Fig. 1. A number of light-emitting diode (LED) APs l = {1, . . . , N l} are placed on the ceiling for simultaneous communication and illumination, while the RF APs f = {1, . . . , N f } are deployed on the walls to provide complementary wireless coverage. On the communication floor, a set of mobile users k = {1, . . . , K },move at different velocities following random waypoint (RWP) mobility patterns. Each user k is equipped with a multi-branch angle diversity receiver for VLC reception and an RF antenna, enabling dynamic technology selection. Each user connects to either the VLC subnetwork or the RF subnetwork, but not both simultaneously. This selection is determined by a binary variable αk ∈ { 0, 1}, where αk = 1 indicates association with the VLC subnetwork and αk = 0 corresponds to the RF subnetwork. Furthermore, an IRS mirror array is mounted on one of the walls and consists of M = {1, . . . , M } mirrors. The area of each mirror is denoted by dA m = hm × wm,where hm and wm represent the height and width of mirror 

m, respectively. A spacing qm is considered between adjacent mirrors to avoid interference during rotation. In addition, the orientation of each rotational mirror can be controlled by two angles: the roll angle φm and the yaw angle ϑm, using micro-electro-mechanical systems (MEMS) technology [5]. This rotation allows each mirror to steer the reflected VLC beam toward intended users and effectively extends the coverage of the VLC subnetwork. A central controller unit coordinates both the VLC and RF subnetworks by collecting channel state information from all APs and users and determines the optimal technology selection for each user.

P ROPOSED INTELLIGENT DRL F RAMEWORK 

The optimization problem in (12) is reformulated as an MDP characterized by the tuple (S, A, R, P, γ ), where S is the global state space, A is the joint action space of both agents, R is the shared cooperative reward encoding the objective of P1 , P is the state transition probability under the joint actions of the agents, and γ ∈ (0 , 1) is the discount factor balancing immediate and long-term rewards. The hy-brid discrete-continuous structure of P1 cannot be efficiently accommodated by conventional DRL approaches. Value-based methods such as DQN [10] operate only over discrete action spaces and would require quantizing the continuous power fractions and mirror angles, causing a combinatorial explosion that is intractable for practical system sizes. Policy-gradient methods such as DDPG [12] support continuous actions, but a single agent must learn a policy spanning the binary selection variables α, the power fractions, and the mirror angles within one heterogeneous action space, which couples variables operating on different scales and physical timescales and degrades convergence stability. Motivated by these limi-tations, the proposed framework decomposes the joint action space across cooperative agents: the selection and power agent 

AG SP operates on the scheduling variables {α, P(v), P(r)}

executed by the central controller, while the IRS orientation agent AG IRS operates on the mirror angles {φ, ϑ} executed by the IRS controller. The agents follow a natural two-timescale ordering in which AG IRS acts first to reshape the VLC channel, after which AG SP observes the updated channel and makes an informed selection decision. To preserve coordination, the agents are trained under centralized training with decentralized execution, where each critic accesses the joint state and both agents’ actions during training while each actor relies only on its local observation at execution. B. Simulation Results 

The performance of our proposed algorithm is evaluated in terms of the sum rate over training episodes as shown in Fig. 2 for K = 6 users. The proposed algorithm converges after approximately 2000 episodes to a stable sum rate of 350 Mbps, within 10% of the exhaustive search upper bound of 392 Mbps. This confirms the near-optimal performance of our learned policy. The deterministic SCA method achieves 330 Mbps and must be re-executed at every scheduling slot. Moreover, our algorithm outperforms the DDPG and DQN by 13% and 25%, respectively. This is because DQN converges fastest due to its simpler discrete action space but saturates early at 280 Mbps, as it cannot capture the continuous action space. Additionally, the DDPG algorithm achieves a higher steady-state rate than DQN but oscillates by 25 Mbps around the SCA benchmark due to the instability of handling the hybrid discrete-continuous action space within a single agent, while the proposed multi-agent DRL algorithm remains stable with oscillations below 5 Mbps. Fig. 3 shows the sum rate for different numbers of users. It can be seen that all approaches initially improve with K until reaching a peak at K = 8 , beyond which the growing inter-user interference becomes the limiting factor. The proposed algorithm consistently outperforms all baselines, achieving a peak sum rate of 415 Mbps. This is because the multi-agents jointly adapt the technology selection, power allocation, and mirror orientation to the instantaneous channel conditions of each user. The deterministic SCA approach provides the second-best performance, as it optimizes all variables but converges to local optima rather than the global policy learned by the DRL agents. The proposed algorithm maintains the highest fairness index above 0.93 even at K = 12 . Finally, VLC only and RF only result in the poorest fairness, as neither can exploit technology diversity to serve users whose preferred subnetwork is temporarily unavailable. VI. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, a joint optimization problem was formu-lated for network selection, power allocation, and IRS mirror orientation in dynamic indoor hybrid RF/VLC networks to maximize the proportional fairness utility, which balances aggregate sum rate and inter-user fairness. Due to the non-convex mixed-integer nature of the optimization problem, it was reformulated as an MDP and solved using a multi-agent DRL algorithm operating under centralized training with decentralized execution, where one agent handles technology selection and power allocation while a second agent continu-ously reconfigures the IRS mirror angles in response to user mobility and LoS blockage. Simulation results demonstrated that the proposed algorithm consistently outperforms other baselines in sum rate and maintains a fairness index above 0.93 at high user densities.
